Four tabs

Explore the app

The same four tabs you find in the app: three public NASA sources and your own archive.

APOD · science.nasa.gov

Today at NASA

The Astronomy Picture of the Day with its title and full explanation, as NASA publishes them, and the credit when NASA gives one.

  • Go back day by day, pick a date or jump to a random day since 1995
  • Full-screen view from the details screen
  • YouTube and Vimeo videos play in the details screen

Mars · mars.nasa.gov

Mars Rovers

Raw images from Curiosity and Perseverance, straight from NASA's mission feeds, sol by sol.

  • Filter by sol and camera
  • Jump to the latest sol in one tap
  • Every photo with its camera, sol and Earth date

EPIC · epic.gsfc.nasa.gov

Earth from EPIC

Full-disk images of Earth taken by NASA's EPIC camera.

  • Pick a date to see that day's images
  • Capture time and coordinates for each image
  • Save them to Photos with their title and credit

Personal archive

Favorites

Keep the images you like from any tab in a private archive on your iPhone.

  • Filter by APOD, Rovers or EPIC
  • Open each one with its full details
  • Stored only on your device

Home Screen

Widgets and a daily notification

Bring NASA to your Home Screen, and get a heads-up when the new picture is out.

  • Astronomy Picture of the DayToday's APOD image, updated every day.
  • Latest from MarsThe newest raw photo from Perseverance or Curiosity, updated every few hours.
  • Earth from EPICThe latest full-disk image of Earth taken by EPIC.

Optional daily notification

NASA Exp now Astronomy Picture of the DayToday's picture is out.

Off by default. Turn it on in the app's About screen and choose the time; tapping it opens Today. All widgets come in small and medium sizes.

Questions

FAQ

Is NASA Exp an official NASA app?

No. NASA Exp is not an official NASA app and is not affiliated with or endorsed by NASA. Images and data come from public NASA services (science.nasa.gov, mars.nasa.gov, api.nasa.gov) and keep their original credits.

How much does it cost?

Nothing. NASA Exp is free, with no ads and no in-app purchases.

What data does it collect?

None. There are no accounts, analytics, ads or tracking, and your favorites and settings stay on your iPhone. The details are in the privacy policy.

Where do the images come from?

The Astronomy Picture of the Day comes from science.nasa.gov, the rover photos from the raw-image feeds on mars.nasa.gov, and the Earth images from NASA's EPIC camera, through api.nasa.gov and epic.gsfc.nasa.gov. The picture of the day shows its credit when NASA gives one.

Do I need an account?

No. Open the app and start exploring.

Which languages does it support?

English and Spanish. Titles and explanations come from NASA and are shown as NASA publishes them, in English.

Does it work on iPad?

NASA Exp is designed for iPhone. There is no iPad version.

Which iOS version do I need?

iOS 17.0 or later.
